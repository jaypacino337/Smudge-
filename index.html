Under the hood

Three transactions, on a timer

There is no magic here and it's deliberately boring. Claiming a pons creator fee and turning it into a burn is three on-chain steps that any wallet can watch happen.

Claim

The creator wallet claims the accrued fee from the pons locker. It's the only wallet that can — which is also why it's the one thing worth protecting properly.

Buy back

The claimed Robinhood Chain ETH buys $ZAZU on its own pool, sliced into TWAP orders so the buy isn't handing most of itself to slippage.

Burn

The bought tokens are transferred to 0x…dEaD, the LP slice is paired back in, and the tx hashes land in the ledger above.

One honest warning

A buyback-and-burn is a policy, not a property of the token. It holds because the creator wallet keeps running it — and if that wallet is a single key on a laptop, that's the actual risk, not the smart contract.

Put the fee wallet behind a multisig, publish the cadence, and let the ledger be the proof. Promising a share of revenue is a securities question rather than a design one; get real advice before you write anything stronger than what's on this page.

Questions

FAQ

Is claiming pons creator fees actually hard?

No. Connect the wallet that created the token to the pons interface and claim — fees arrive as ETH on Robinhood Chain. Only the creator wallet can do it, and pons automation will route unclaimed fees to the creator payout wallet anyway.

The buyback and burn on top is three transactions in a loop. The hard parts are operational: protecting the creator key, sizing claims so gas doesn't eat them, and actually running the cadence every day.

What's the fee split?

The pool takes 1% of each trade. Of that, 70% goes to the creator and 30% to pons on the current factory — tokens launched from the legacy factory keep the original 90/10. Check which factory yours came from before you quote a number.

Why not just point the fee wallet at a bot?

You can — several services claim, buy back and burn automatically. The trade-off is that pointing your fee wallet at a third party means trusting it with the whole fee stream. Read what's actually enforced on-chain versus what's just their server before you route anything.

Are the numbers on this page real?

Not until assets/js/fees.js is switched from source: 'demo' to 'chain' with an RPC and contract addresses filled in. While it's on demo the dashboard shows a warning badge and zeroed totals, on purpose — invented buyback figures on a token site are a real legal problem, not a cosmetic one.

How do I change the art?

Everything comes out of generator/tools/draw-zazu.js. The ELEMENTS array at the top is the whole panel — add an entry and you get a new Zazu, a new card, and a new favicon-safe render on the next run. Drop a real photo at assets/zazu-source.png and it uses that as the base instead.
